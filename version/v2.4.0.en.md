# v2.4.0 (`add_states` branch)

All changes in this version are on the `add_states` branch, maintained separately from the main line `paper-verfication`'s
per-scenario BC policy (v2.3.0: no BC on lt, BC kept on eq/gt) — to remove BC as a confounder,
this branch uses no BC on eq/gt/lt and focuses on state/reward design.

## Change 1: `bottleneck_risk` state feature (all env.py of eq/gt/lt)

The observation gains one aggregate "approaching a dead end" signal — the share of remaining services with
`valid_ecu_count<=1`; previously the policy could only infer this implicitly from the raw per-service
`svc_valid_ecus` array. lt grows from 5N+6+2M to 5N+7+2M dimensions, eq/gt likewise.

**Effect (lt, one 500k-step run, no BC)**: success_rate 80.0%→85.0%,
AR 0.6005→0.6199; both metrics move the same way but only modestly (+5pp), no multiple seeds.
The contribution on eq/gt is smaller (AR about +0.004~0.006), as expected — these two scenarios already have success_rate
capped at 100%, so the "dead-end risk" barely exists there.

## Change 2: AR-proportional terminal reward ported from lt to eq/gt

Found that eq/gt's `ppo_mask/env.py` still used the old binary reward of v1.1.0
(`+M`/`-M`, ignoring AR), while lt had switched to `M*ar`/`-M` back in v2.2.0.
The training curves show eq/gt's valid_placed equal to M from step=0 (these two scenarios have N>=M,
so completing the task is not hard), but the episode AR curve is almost flat over 2M steps — the root cause is that
the reward did not distinguish "how well" anything was placed, so PPO had no gradient to learn from.

After porting the `M*ar` (no violations) / `-M` (violations) reward to eq/gt (same no BC, with the
bottleneck feature, 2M steps):

| Scenario | Setting | success_rate | AR mean | ILP AR | AR gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| eq | old binary reward (no BC, with feature) | 100% | 0.4623 | 0.5321 | 0.0698 |
| eq | **AR reward** (no BC, with feature) | 100% | **0.5285** | 0.5321 | **0.0036** |
| gt | old binary reward (no BC, with feature) | 100% | 0.4647 | 0.6337 | 0.1690 |
| gt | **AR reward** (no BC, with feature) | 100% | **0.5815** | 0.6337 | **0.0522** |

**This is the largest single change found so far** — the AR gap shrinks to 1/5 to 1/30 of before,
far more than BC and the bottleneck feature combined. Mechanism: these two scenarios already have success_rate
always 100%, so the old reward gave every episode the same constant +M and PPO's advantage
was almost pure noise with no real gradient direction; once AR enters the reward, the advantage finally
varies with "how well things are placed" and the gradient becomes meaningful.

## Known issue: one experiment was contaminated and must be re-validated

While checking the effect of BC, the procedure `git stash push` (remove a change) → start training in the background →
`sleep N` seconds → `git stash pop` (restore the change) was used; once
(lt, 5M steps, the supposed "no BC, no feature" control) `sleep 2` was very likely too short
(importing Python + torch/numpy/gymnasium often takes more than 2 seconds), so the training process most likely read the
restored "with feature" version instead of the intended "no feature" version — evidence: a separately
re-run "5M steps + feature" result (success_rate=70.0%, AR mean=0.6347,
std=0.0799, last50=0.6661) matches the "control" numbers of that time to four decimal places,
which on CPU with a fixed seed is only possible with the same code.

**Conclusion**: the v2.3.0 figure "lt without BC 47.5%→70.0%" should still hold
(the BC switch was a clean control and did not involve this risky stash procedure), but the claim that "the bottleneck feature
contributes nothing at 5M steps" is unreliable and must be re-run safely (before stash pop,
confirm the training log already shows `[train]` instead of relying on a fixed sleep) as a
clean control.

## Next steps

- Re-run a clean lt 5M-step "no BC, no feature" control to fix the contaminated data above.
- Test the hypothesis "the AR reward may slightly lower success_rate by encouraging tight packing" —
  needs a clean "old binary reward vs AR reward" control on lt (fixed
  5M steps / no BC / no feature); no data can answer it yet.
- Delete the dead line `step_reward` in `env.py` (computed but never added to `reward`,
  a leftover from the v1.1.0 switch to sparse reward that suggests dense shaping that isn't there).
- Explore potential-based reward shaping (using `bottleneck_risk` as the potential
  `Φ(s)=-β·bottleneck_risk`), which in theory spreads the signal "will this cause a deadlock later" from the end to every step
  without changing the set of optimal policies.
