# v2.0.0

## Background

v1.2.2 introduced best-of-N random resampling at evaluation to ease the
success_rate ceiling of an "online, non-backtracking policy", but `EVAL_BEST_OF_N=8` at the time was **an untuned initial default**
(v1.2.2's own note: "N=8 is the initial default and has not been tuned"). All later
lt success_rate numbers (v1.2.2~v1.2.4, including v1.2.4's multi-seed diagnosis)
were measured at N=8, leading to the conclusion "success_rate is stuck at 60%~67%, a
systematic bottleneck".

That conclusion was never validated — nobody had asked "has N=8 already hit the top?"

## Finding of this version: N=8 is far from saturated; success_rate keeps rising sharply with N

Reusing the trained seed=42 model from v1.2.4's multi-seed diagnosis
(`results/paper-verfication/lt/ppo_mask/0e1a36bb_bc/model_0e1a36bb_bc_v1.2.4`),
without retraining, [src/scripts/eval_bestofn_sweep.py](../scripts/eval_bestofn_sweep.py)
re-runs best-of-N evaluation on the same 40 held-out test scenarios, with N = 8/16/32/64/128:

| N | success_rate | ar_mean | ar_std | avg_attempts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8   | 0.5250 | 0.6095 | 0.0771 | 4.65 |
| 16  | 0.6750 | 0.6199 | 0.0778 | 7.42 |
| 32  | 0.7750 | 0.6143 | 0.0681 | 10.65 |
| 64  | 0.8000 | 0.6122 | 0.0710 | 16.35 |
| 128 | 0.8500 | 0.6104 | 0.0740 | 31.27 |
| 256 | 0.8500 | 0.6108 | 0.0676 | 50.02 |
| 512 | 0.8750 | 0.6122 | 0.0702 | 85.08 |
| 1024 | 0.9500 | 0.6186 | 0.0737 | 91.62 |

**success_rate rises from 52.5% at N=8 all the way to 95% at N=1024, a gain of 42.5
percentage points.** The curve briefly flattens at N=128→256 (both 0.85), but jumps another
7.5 points at N=512→1024, so it has not truly converged to a hard ceiling — after 1024 attempts
2 of the 40 test scenarios have never succeeded; these two are most likely the truly structurally unplaceable cases,
and all the others are essentially "not enough attempts". This further confirms the core conclusion of v2.0.0:
most of the feasibility gap caused by "online, non-backtracking" can be closed by more resampling; the earlier
"~65% is a systematic bottleneck" conclusion was an artifact of "N=8 is too small", not a hard ceiling.

Side observation: the N=8 success_rate measured this time (0.525) does not match the number recorded for
seed=42 in v1.2.4's multi-seed diagnosis (0.650) — evaluation uses `deterministic=False`
random sampling, so repeated runs with the same N have considerable variance; even the historical number for the single point
"N=8" cannot be taken as a stable value.

Per-scenario comparison data for the 40 scenarios (ILP_AR / demand-capacity ratio / PPO@N=1024/8192 results) is in
[v2.0.0_scenario_detail.md](v2.0.0_scenario_detail.md).

## Wording correction: this is not "RL failed", it is a usable scheme with a clear boundary

v1.2.2~v1.2.4 repeatedly described lt with failure-leaning wording such as "systematic bottleneck" and "success_rate won't go up";
the data of this version require correcting this:

- **Single-try policy (N=1, deterministic)**: clearly worse than the ILP; this is a limitation of the policy itself
  and need not be hidden.
- **Policy + best-of-N resampling (as one system)**: 95% over the 40 scenarios, and
  the vast majority (38/40) do not need a blindly large N. This is a genuinely usable scheme close to the ILP,
  not a "failure".
- **The remaining 2/40 (5%)**: cannot be placed even with 8192 chances — this is the honest boundary of the current scheme,
  not evidence of "overall failure".

The accurate characterization: "RL + best-of-N" works in the vast majority of scenarios and has a known, quantifiable boundary in a few
extremely high-utilization scenarios; it is neither an unconditional success nor a failure.

**Risk note (not mentioned in earlier docs)**: best-of-N is resampling at evaluation/inference time
and does not change the trained policy — essentially "the same policy may retry the same problem,
and one hit counts", exploiting the policy's own random sampling, not a stronger policy. This costs nothing in offline
evaluation, where N=8192 is fine; but if the policy is deployed online,
whether "retry N times" is feasible depends on the real cost of a failure — if a failure can be retried immediately
(pure computational simulation), best-of-N fully holds; if every "placement" itself occupies real resources,
retrying a thousand times may be unacceptable in practice. This must be settled before discussing whether
best-of-N is the final scheme.

## Why this is a major version

This is not a tuning or feature addition; it **overturns a premise that the previous three versions (v1.2.2/v1.2.3/v1.2.4)
relied on repeatedly** — "lt success_rate has hit a systematic ceiling".
All later decisions based on this premise (whether to change the training-side structure, reward shaping, curriculum
learning, etc.) must be re-evaluated, so a new major version marks this turning point instead of
a minor increment.

## Next steps (no change in this version, left for the next)

- The `EVAL_BEST_OF_N` default (currently 8, in `scenarios/{eq,gt,lt}/{ppo_mask,ppo_lagrangian}/config.py`)
  needs a re-evaluated value, weighing the acceptable compute cost of evaluation — avg_attempts
  grows quickly with N (91.62 at N=1024), meaning 44% of the scenarios use almost all retries before succeeding or giving up;
  evaluation time does not pay off linearly, so a balance between cost and benefit is needed,
  not blindly the maximum.
- v1.2.4's multi-seed diagnosis ("success_rate mean ≈0.640, small variance, systematic
  bottleneck") is based on N=8 and must be reproduced with multiple seeds once the new N is settled.
- Only one (scenario, algorithm) combination, `lt/ppo_mask`, has been validated; whether `ppo_lagrangian` and the
  other algorithms have the same N sensitivity is untested.
- The roughly 2 scenarios "unplaceable even with 1024 attempts" need to be characterized (tightness / share of large items)
  to confirm they are truly structural hard cases, instead of blindly increasing N.
