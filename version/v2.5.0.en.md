# v2.5.0 (`add_states` branch) — wrap-up of the reward-engineering exploration + final fallback data

## Background

After v2.4.0, to address "lt success_rate / AR stuck in a band", five consecutive independent, theoretically motivated
changes in the reward design space were tried, each validated with
multiple seeds (8~10), not single runs. All of them point to the same conclusion.

## Summary of the five reward experiments (lt, 500k-step ablation unless noted)

| # | Change | success_rate | AR mean | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Baseline: M\*ar reward, no entropy annealing | 86.50% ± 5.68 | 0.6207 ± 0.0074 | — |
| 1 | bottleneck_risk potential shaping (discrete threshold → continuous, several fixed/annealed β) | 85~87% (10 seeds) | 0.6207~0.6208 | no effect |
| 2 | reward stretched from M\*ar to M\*(2ar-1) (equal weight for AR and violations) + real entropy annealing (0.02→0.002) | 87.19% ± 6.19 | 0.6276 ± 0.0100 | no effect (within noise) |
| 3 | failure penalty changed from a fixed -M to graded by valid_placed/M | 85.62% ± 5.13 | 0.6210 ± 0.0132 | no effect |
| 4 | shaping potential changed from bottleneck_risk to an FFD greedy feasibility simulation (trigger rate 0.13%→99.8%) | 85.50% ± 4.53 | 0.6208 ± 0.0134 | no effect |

**Five mechanisms with widely different signal strengths and design ideas all converge to the same band**:
success_rate 80~90%, AR 0.60~0.65, regardless of how the reward is tuned.

## Conclusion

Within the framework of "RL learns entirely through the reward", a success_rate of 80~90% and
an AR of 0.60~0.65 are the real current capability limit of this online, single-step, non-backtracking decision structure,
not a reward that was never found.

Using a solver (full bipartite matching / ILP) to mask beyond this limit was discussed
and explicitly rejected — it would make success_rate a structural guarantee rather than a learning result, contrary to
the research goal of "RL learning a policy by itself that reaches the optimum every time" (equivalent to the previously
discussed and rejected "ILP fallback" scheme, just hidden somewhere else). Using solver signals for
reward shaping (rather than masking) is consistent with the principle (the solver only provides a more accurate
teaching signal during training; the policy still has to learn to respond, and deployment does not depend on the solver); round 4,
FFD feasibility shaping, was the experiment under this principle, and it also had zero effect.

**This reward-engineering line ends here.** This does not mean "RL failed"; it means that under the current
decision structure this is the honest capability limit, worth recording as it is rather than hiding or prettifying it with more reward
tuning.

## History

- This is not the first time the project tried reward shaping with a feasibility signal: v0.3.0/v0.3.1
  (2026-07-03) tried almost the same idea (the `_feasibility` potential function), and a single
  run (no multiple seeds) concluded "lt success_rate 42.5%→40.0%, no improvement",
  rolled back in v0.3.2. This time it was re-validated with multiple seeds and a more precise signal, a
  more rigorous reproduction of the same hypothesis, with the same conclusion (no effect), but this time properly validated, not guessed.
- BC policy split: see [[v2.3.0]]; the bottleneck_risk feature and the first shaping: see [[v2.4.0]].
